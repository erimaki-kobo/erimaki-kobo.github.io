Answers for LLM inference engineers

Why do vLLM or llama.cpp logprobs differ from Transformers, and how much is normal?

Two implementations that both run float32 agree to about 1e-5 nats per token. With fp16 on one side the gap is about 1e-3 on average and 1e-2 at worst; with bf16 it is about 1e-2 on average, and single tokens can be off by 0.1 or more. Those figures come from the measurements on this page and line up with what users have reported for vLLM on GPUs.

A gap larger than the precision explains is usually a setting, not arithmetic. The two sides tokenize the prompt differently, or one returns processed logprobs and the other raw ones, or a server default applies a repetition penalty, or half precision enters through a default: vLLM loads float32 checkpoints in half precision (bf16 on Ampere and newer GPUs since v0.9.1, fp16 on older ones), and llama.cpp's flash attention casts keys and values to F16 even for an F32 model. The tables below list each cause with the version it was verified on, and lpcheck.py tells you which one you have.

How much logprob difference is normal?

About 1e-6 to 1e-5 nats per token in float32, about 1e-3 in fp16 and about 1e-2 in bf16. The numbers are the absolute difference in the log-probability of the same token given the same token ids, in nats. A second yardstick comes from NVIDIA's NeMo RL, which accepts a new model when the mean of exp(|Δlogprob|) between Hugging Face and vLLM stays at or below 1.05 at equal precision, and reports 1.01 to 1.04 for its own runs. On the measurements here that statistic is 1.000001 for float32, 1.002 for fp16, 1.017 for bf16 and 1.030 for Q8_0.

fp16 keeps 10 mantissa bits and bf16 keeps 7, which is why bf16 comes out about nine times worse here. Qi et al. measured the same direction in RL fine-tuning of a 1.5B model, where fp16 made the mismatch between training and inference engines roughly 24 times smaller than bf16, and vLLM's own FAQ suggests float16 over bfloat16 when stable logprobs matter.

Small gaps still change text. Against the float64 reference, float32 reproduced all 64 greedy tokens on 8 of 8 prompts, fp16 on 7 of 8 and bf16 on 1 of 8. The fp16 flips all sat where the reference's top two candidates were within 0.010 nats of each other. In bf16 the largest margin that flipped was 0.24 nats, about three times the 99th-percentile gap. So "the outputs differ at temperature 0" is not by itself evidence of a bug. A flip where the reference separates its top two tokens by much more than the noise on neighbouring tokens is.

What does "logprob" mean on each engine?

vLLM and llama.cpp return raw model log-probabilities by default. SGLang returns them after penalties and, when sampling, after temperature. Transformers gives either, depending on the field you read. Before comparing numbers, check that both sides compute the same quantity. Each row was read in the source of the version shown.

EngineReturned by defaultSwitch
vLLM v0.33.0Raw. log_softmax of the model's logits, taken before penalties, logit bias, temperature and top-k/top-p. The model produces the logits in its own dtype; the softmax is taken in float32.--logprobs-mode raw_logprobs | processed_logprobs | raw_logits | processed_logits. SGLang v0.5.23Processed. Logit bias and penalties are applied to the logits first. With greedy decoding the result is log_softmax(logits); with sampling it is the log of softmax(logits / temperature).Environment variable SGLANG_RETURN_ORIGINAL_LOGPROB returns the values before temperature scaling.

Transformers 5.22.2model(ids).logits is raw. In generate(), output_logits=True returns raw logits and output_scores=True returns the processed scores.compute_transition_scores() works on the processed scores.

The practical consequence: a logprob from vLLM at temperature 0.7 is comparable with log_softmax(logits) from Transformers. The same request to SGLang returns log_softmax(logits / 0.7), so the reference has to divide by the temperature too.

The difference has cost real pipelines time. When ServiceNow moved an RL pipeline from vLLM 0.8.5 (V0 engine) to 0.18.1 (V1), the first fix needed to match the old training curves was logprobs-mode: processed_logprobs; turning prefix caching and async scheduling off came next, and computing the output head in float32 closed the last gap.

Measured: the same weights in three implementations

The model is TinyStories 15M (stories15M.bin from llama2.c, sha256 cd590644…f49a), a 6-layer Llama-architecture model with a 32,000-token vocabulary. It is small enough to run in float64. The reference is a NumPy forward pass in float64 written from the architecture alone (np_ref.py), so it does not share code with any engine. The same weights were loaded into Transformers 5.22.2 (torch 2.15.0, CPU) and converted with llama.cpp's convert_hf_to_gguf.py for llama-server b12270 (official Linux x64 build, CPU with AVX-512, 2 threads). Eight prompts, 64 greedy tokens each, top-5 logprobs at every position, all fed as token ids.

Three things stand out.

  1. An F32 GGUF is not a float32 run by default. With default flags the F32 model lands in the same range as the F16 model. Switching attention to F32 (-fa off -ctk f32 -ctv f32) brings the shape of the distribution within 2.6e-5 of float64. Changing only the cache type has no effect, because flash attention casts K and V to F16 on its own (src/llama-graph.cpp); the two configurations gave identical numbers.
  2. What remains is a constant per position. In the all-F32 run, the whole 1.0e-3 gap is an offset shared by the five top tokens at a position. Summing the probabilities the server returned for all 32,000 tokens gave 0.99904 at one position and 1.00018 at another, the same amounts as the offsets there. The server accumulates its normalizer in a float (get_token_probabilities() in tools/server/server-common.cpp), and thousands of the terms are near or below the rounding step of the running sum. Re-adding the same terms with a float32 running sum in NumPy reproduced the offsets at three positions to three digits (−9.65e-4, −6.87e-4, +1.84e-4). Token ranking and gaps between tokens are unaffected.
  3. The engine disagrees with itself along two paths. With default flags, the logprob of a token produced during generation and the logprob of the same token when the same prefix is sent as a prompt differ by up to 0.025 nats. With attention in F32 the two paths agree to 5.0e-6. The llama.cpp README says as much about cache_prompt: logits are not guaranteed to be identical between prompt processing and token generation.

Not measured here: vLLM and SGLang (no GPU on the test machine), and models larger than 15M parameters. K-quants are missing because llama-quantize falls back from them when the hidden size (288 here) is not a multiple of 256. The rows are also available as JSON, and the viewer below shows four reports from the same setup taken against the Transformers float32 reference.

Check your own setup with lpcheck.py

lpcheck.py is one Python file. Against an endpoint it needs only the standard library; with a Hugging Face reference it needs torch and transformers. It sends token ids, so tokenization cannot leak into the numeric comparison, and it has the candidate score the reference's own continuation, so every position is compared on identical context even after greedy outputs split.

curl -O https://erimaki-kobo.github.io/en/engine-logprob-parity-check/lpcheck.py

# Transformers (float32) as the reference, a running server as the candidate
python lpcheck.py --ref-hf Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B --base-url http://localhost:8000/v1

# build the reference where the GPU is, test somewhere else
python lpcheck.py --ref-hf ./model --save-ref ref.json
python lpcheck.py --ref-file ref.json --base-url http://localhost:8080/v1 --json report.json

# two servers: old version against new, or vLLM against llama.cpp
python lpcheck.py --ref-url http://localhost:8000/v1 --base-url http://localhost:8001/v1

# no server at all: what does bf16 cost this model?
python lpcheck.py --ref-hf ./model --cand-hf ./model --cand-dtype bfloat16

Are my endpoint's logprobs raw or processed?

This runs step P from the browser: seven one-token requests to /completions. Requests go straight from this page to the URL you enter.

A network error means the server refused a cross-origin request or is a plain http:// host other than localhost. llama.cpp accepts any origin by default. vLLM v0.32.0 and later refuse cross-origin requests unless started with --allowed-origins '["https://erimaki-kobo.github.io"]'; v0.31 and earlier accepted them. The command-line script has no such limit.

How do I read the verdict?

IDENTICAL
Every compared logprob matched to the last printed digit. Expect this only between two instances of the same build on the same hardware.
WITHIN_TOLERANCE
Same tokens in, same meaning, mean exp(|Δlogprob|) at or below the tolerance (1.05 unless you change --max-mult-err), and every top-1 flip at a near-tie. Compare the printed mean with the table at the top to see which precision the gap corresponds to. For a float32-against-float32 check, tighten the tolerance: --max-mult-err 1.0001.
NUMERIC_GAP
The inputs and the meaning match, and the numbers still disagree by more than the tolerance, or a token flipped where the reference's margin was more than four times the 99th-percentile gap. Look at the weights first (quantization, revision, an adapter left on), then at the dtype of each side, then at the engine version.
NOT_THE_SAME_COMPUTATION
One or more of: different token ids for the same text, values that are not log-probabilities, a temperature-like slope, a penalty inside the logprobs, a token chosen at temperature 0 that is not the top-1, or a one-position shift. Each is a setting. The report still prints the numeric gap with ids pinned, so you can see whether anything is left after the setting is fixed.

How do I make two engines agree?

  1. Pin the input. Send token ids. If production sends text, fix the BOS flag and the chat template until step T passes.
  2. Neutralize the sampler. Temperature 0, repetition penalty 1, no logit bias. On vLLM start with --generation-config vllm so the model repo cannot add defaults.
  3. Make the meaning match. vLLM returns raw logprobs unless --logprobs-mode says otherwise. For SGLang at a temperature other than 1, divide the reference logits by the temperature or set SGLANG_RETURN_ORIGINAL_LOGPROB. On llama.cpp leave post_sampling_probs off. In Transformers use the logits, not the scores.
  4. Match the precision. Load both sides in the same dtype. For a strict test use float32 on both: --dtype float32 on vLLM, -fa off -ctk f32 -ctv f32 on llama.cpp.
  5. Compare the same path. Scoring-path numbers against a full forward pass, decode-path numbers against cached generation.
  6. Accept the floor. After all of that, 1e-5 in float32, 1e-2 in fp16 and 1e-1 in bf16 on single tokens are what the arithmetic leaves. Exact agreement needs the two sides to share kernels: the vLLM team reports bitwise-identical rollout and training logprobs after making the training forward pass call vLLM's own inference kernels.

What can this not tell you?

  • The measured table is one 15M-parameter model on one CPU. GPU kernels, deeper models and MoE routing change the magnitudes. Run the tool on your own model for your own floor.
  • The llama.cpp and Transformers paths of lpcheck were run against the real software. The vLLM path (prompt_logprobs, return_tokens_as_token_ids, /tokenize) and the SGLang path were written from the v0.33.0 and v0.5.23 source and tested against a mock server that mimics their response shapes. They have not been run against a live vLLM or SGLang server. If a real response differs, the tool reports request errors or falls back to step-by-step scoring.
  • Servers that return token text without ids (SGLang's OpenAI-compatible API) are matched by text, which can miss byte-fragment tokens. Those positions are counted and reported.
  • The KL figure uses the top-k tokens plus one bucket for the rest, so it is a lower bound on the true divergence.
  • Step-by-step scoring on a server with a prefix cache measures the cached path. That is the reason step C exists.
  • The 1.05 tolerance is a convention borrowed from NeMo RL, not a property of floating point. For RL, where a ratio of probabilities multiplies every gradient, the tail matters more than the mean.

Questions

Is it normal for vLLM logprobs to differ from Hugging Face Transformers?

Yes, within the precision of the run. Different kernels round differently, so two correct implementations of the same model do not return identical floats. In float32 the gap is around 1e-6 to 1e-5 nats per token; in fp16 around 1e-3; in bf16 around 1e-2 with occasional tokens off by 0.1 or more. A gap well beyond the row for your precision points at a setting or at different weights.

How much logprob difference is acceptable?

It depends on what you do with the numbers. For evaluation and ranking, gaps of the size your precision explains are fine, because token gaps move far less than absolute values. NVIDIA NeMo RL treats a mean exp(|difference|) up to 1.05 between Hugging Face and vLLM as acceptable at equal precision. For reinforcement learning, where a ratio of probabilities multiplies every gradient, a few tokens with large ratios matter more than the mean.

Why does vLLM give different text than Transformers at temperature 0?

Usually for one of four reasons: the prompt became different token ids (BOS, chat template); vLLM applied sampling defaults from generation_config.json, such as a repetition penalty, that your Transformers call did not; the two ran in different dtypes (vLLM runs float32 checkpoints in half precision by default: bf16 on Ampere and newer GPUs, fp16 on older ones); or the logits differ by rounding and a near-tie tipped the other way. Greedy decoding amplifies the last one: after the first different token the two texts are different continuations.

Are vLLM logprobs computed before or after temperature and top-p?

Before, by default. The V1 engine returns log_softmax of the raw logits, taken before penalties, temperature and top-k/top-p. Start the server with --logprobs-mode processed_logprobs to get the values after all processors. The older V0 engine returned processed values, which is why numbers changed for some users after upgrading.

Why are llama.cpp logprobs off by the same amount for every token at a position?

The server converts logits to probabilities with a float32 running sum over the whole vocabulary. The sum is slightly off, so every token at that position shifts by one constant: up to about 1e-3 nats in the measurement on this page. Differences between tokens at the same position are unaffected.

Does this page see my prompts, reports or API key?

No. The page is static. The report viewer reads the file in your browser, and the probe sends requests from your browser directly to the base URL you enter. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere else.
